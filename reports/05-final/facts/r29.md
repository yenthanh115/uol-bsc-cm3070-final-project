# R29 — Baseline comparison is interpreted

**Requirement:** Baseline comparison is interpreted
**Acceptance / validation criterion:** Explain not merely which model scores higher, but what
the difference implies about the modelling problem.
**Priority:** MUST

**Verdict: PASS**

The report does not stop at ranking the models; it interprets what the baseline-vs-main
differences *mean* for the surge-prediction problem. The Logistic Regression baseline is
compared against the Random Forest / XGBoost main models and against single-feature baselines,
and the resulting gaps are explained in terms of the problem's structure: data density versus
model complexity, why boosting fails under scarce positives, why the linear baseline lags, what
a calibration-not-discrimination gap implies, and what the sparse-community transfer advantage
says about generalisation. This satisfies the "what the difference implies" clause, not just
the "which scores higher" clause.

## Evidence from the report (`final-report-v0.0.2.md`)

### The comparison is quantified first (the "which scores higher" part)
- [@tbl:perf-default]/[@tbl:perf-tuned] give LR vs RF vs XGB with 95% CIs; [@tbl:baselines]
  adds the random and single-feature baselines; [@tbl:mcnemar] establishes the differences are
  statistically significant.

### The differences are then interpreted (the "what it implies" part)
- **Complexity vs data density** ([@sec:complexity-density]): the inversion — XGBoost best on
  dense WSB, Random Forest best on sparse pennystocks — is explained by three mechanisms
  (boosting needs enough positives or it fits noise; RF spreads splits across features; XGB
  probability calibration saturates under extreme imbalance). The take-away drawn about the
  *problem* is explicit: "below roughly 5,000 positive training instances Random Forest was the
  more robust choice than gradient boosting," and (Key Findings) "the gap between datasets …
  is larger than the gap between any two models," i.e. **data density constrains performance
  more than model complexity**.
- **Baseline (LR) vs main models**: the linear baseline trails the tree ensembles, and the
  report reads this as evidence the surge signal is not merely additive — non-linear feature
  interactions carry real information ([@sec:modelling-review] frames LR precisely as the test
  of additivity; the realised gap is interpreted accordingly).
- **Multi-feature vs single-feature baseline** ([@tbl:baselines], Statistical Validation):
  +0.087 AUC on WSB and +0.162 on pennystocks are interpreted as "multi-feature integration
  successfully captures complex signal interactions," turning an otherwise weak signal into a
  viable one — a statement about the problem, not a ranking.
- **Calibration, not discrimination**: where models rank well (high AUC) but score poorly at
  the default threshold, the report interprets this as a base-rate/calibration property of the
  problem rather than a model-quality difference (Model Performance; [@sec:operational-precision]).
- **Baseline comparison as a stated objective**: O2 ("Compare … whether more complex models
  improve prediction over simpler baselines") is judged Met with the interpretation that
  "complexity helps under abundance but inverts under scarcity" ([@tbl:objective-verdicts],
  [@sec:eval-objectives]).

### Design framing pre-commits to interpretation, not just scoring
[@sec:modelling-review] sets up the baseline explicitly so its comparison is meaningful: "if
[LR] performs well, the surge signal is largely additive; if a more complex model beats it, the
margin quantifies what non-linear structure is worth." The results then deliver exactly that
interpretation, closing the loop.

## Evidence from the codebase (`src/`)

R29 is an interpretation requirement, but the code produces the quantities the interpretation
rests on, so the comparison is grounded rather than asserted:

- `evaluation.evaluate_baselines` computes the random and single-feature baselines behind
  [@tbl:baselines].
- `evaluation.mcnemar_pairwise_test` produces the pairwise significance ([@tbl:mcnemar]) that
  licenses claims the model differences are real, not noise.
- `evaluation.compute_bootstrap_ci` produces the 95% CIs that let the report note where
  baseline and main-model intervals overlap (e.g. RF vs XGB on pennystocks) — the basis for the
  cautious, problem-focused interpretation rather than an overclaimed ranking.
- `training.py` (LR/RF/XGB via one path) and `run_cross_validation.py` (transfer) generate the
  same-conditions comparison and the cross-community numbers the interpretation draws on.
