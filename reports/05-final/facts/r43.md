# R43 — Evaluation is broader than metric reporting

**Requirement:** Evaluation covers major project issues and critically analyses results relative to objectives—the rubric explicitly separates these criteria.
**Priority:** CRITICAL
**Assessed:** 2026-09-05
**Verdict:** **PASS**

R43 is a CRITICAL requirement with two rubric-separated prongs: (a) the evaluation must
**cover major project issues** (not just report metrics), and (b) it must **critically
analyse results relative to the objectives**. The report's Evaluation section (§5) satisfies
both. It is not a metrics dump: it opens by returning to the three objectives, reports
metrics with uncertainty and significance, then devotes an entire **Critical Analysis**
subsection to interpreting *why* the results look as they do and what they mean for the
project's aims, before closing with evidence-based limitations. The supporting analyses
(cross-dataset transfer, sensitivity sweeps, error analysis) are backed by artefacts already
confirmed on disk in the R36/R28/R26 assessments.

---

## 1. Prong A — the evaluation covers the major project issues ✔

The Evaluation section addresses the substantive issues of the project, well beyond headline
metrics:

- **Problem difficulty / class imbalance** (`§Dataset and Target Analysis`): states the sub-1%
  positive rate (102:1 WSB, 105:1 pennystocks), why accuracy is uninformative, and why
  ranking metrics are essential.
- **Statistical robustness** (`@sec:statistical-validation`): 1,000-resample bootstrap 95% CIs
  on every metric and McNemar's pairwise tests (Bonferroni-corrected), so differences are
  quantified with uncertainty and significance, not asserted.
- **Baselines** (`@tbl:baselines`): random baseline + eleven single-feature LR baselines,
  establishing multi-feature lift (+0.087 WSB, +0.162 pennystocks).
- **Generalisation** (`§Cross-Dataset Transfer`, `@tbl:transfer`, `@sec:cross-community`):
  cross-community transfer both directions, with the asymmetry analysed.
- **Feature contribution** (`§Feature Importance`, `@sec:sentiment-signal`): permutation +
  built-in importances, with caution about the sentiment/target circularity.
- **Sensitivity** (`@sec:sentiment-contribution`, `@tbl:weight-sensitivity`): sentiment-weight
  sweep and Phase 1 vs Phase 2, isolating sentiment's contribution.
- **Error analysis** (`@sec:error-analysis`): false negatives vs false positives dissected by
  cause (cold-start surges; precursor-but-no-surge; extraction noise).
- **Operational usefulness** (`@sec:operational-precision`, `@tbl:acceptance-eval`): best model
  scored against the acceptance criteria (AUC, recall, precision, alert volume) with a
  per-user-scenario verdict.
- **Temporal stability / distribution shift** (`@sec:temporal-stability`): the validation-test
  F1 gap interpreted as calibration drift, not model failure.

## 2. Prong B — results are critically analysed relative to objectives ✔

This is the prong the rubric separates, and the report treats it explicitly:

- **Objectives revisited up front** (`@sec:eval-objectives`, `@tbl:objective-verdicts`): each of
  O1 (predict), O2 (compare), O3 (temporal validity) gets a verdict (all "Met") *with a pointer
  to the supporting evidence*, not a bare claim.
- **Interpretation, not just scores**, throughout the **Critical Analysis** subsection:
  - `@sec:complexity-density`: explains *why* XGB leads on WSB but RF leads on pennystocks
    (boosting needs positives; RF spreads splits; XGB calibration degrades under imbalance) —
    directly answering O2's "does complexity help?".
  - `@sec:sentiment-signal`: reconciles sentiment's weak standalone AUC with its dominant
    permutation importance (interaction effect), and flags the target/feature circularity.
  - `@sec:operational-precision`: converts metrics into an operational verdict against
    `@tbl:acceptance-criteria` (recall target not met; ranking/precision met), i.e. results
    judged against the project's own success criteria.
  - `@sec:temporal-stability`: distinguishes ranking stability (AUC holds) from threshold
    miscalibration (F1 collapses), which is the crux of O3.
- **Honest negative findings**: the system fails the recall criterion and XGB predicts zero
  positives at 0.5 on pennystocks; the report states these plainly rather than hiding them,
  which is exactly the "critical" analysis the rubric rewards.

## Verdict

**R43: PASS.** The Evaluation section is broader than metric reporting on both rubric-separated
prongs. It **covers the major project issues** — class imbalance, statistical robustness
(bootstrap CIs, McNemar), baselines, cross-community generalisation, feature contribution,
sensitivity analysis, error analysis, operational usefulness, and temporal stability — and it
**critically analyses results relative to the objectives**, opening with explicit per-objective
verdicts (`@tbl:objective-verdicts`) and devoting a full Critical Analysis subsection to
*interpreting* the results (why complexity helps or hurts by data density, why sentiment is
interactive, why the operating point fails the recall criterion, why the F1 collapse is
calibration drift rather than model failure). It reports negative findings honestly and grounds
every strand in produced artefacts. This is analysis against the project's aims, not a metrics
dump.

### Minor observations (not blocking)

- PR-AUC is discussed qualitatively (~0.14, "14× above random" in `@sec:operational-precision`)
  and named as a secondary metric, but is not tabulated per-model alongside AUC/precision/recall/F1.
  Adding a PR-AUC column would round out the metric table; it does not affect the R43 verdict,
  which concerns breadth and critical interpretation rather than any single metric.
- The richest critical analysis (error analysis, operational precision, temporal stability) is
  concentrated on `WSB`; pennystocks is analysed mainly through the density/complexity and
  transfer lenses. This is reasonable given pennystocks' 31-surge fragility (itself flagged in
  the limitations), and does not narrow the evaluation's overall breadth.
