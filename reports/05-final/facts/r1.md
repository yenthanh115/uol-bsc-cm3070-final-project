## 1. What is predicted — a binary per-ticker surge ✔

The prediction target is stated precisely and repeatedly:

- **Abstract:** "predicts whether discussion around an individual stock ticker will surge
  during the following 24 hours."
- **§ Prediction Target (`@sec:prediction-target`):** targets *surges* — "significant
  short-term spikes in both **posting volume** and **sentiment intensity** for a ticker
  within a 24-hour window ... prediction becomes binary classification."
- **§ Surge Definition (`@sec:surge-definition`):** gives the exact deterministic rule — a
  composite of normalised volume growth $\Delta V$ and sentiment-shift magnitude
  $\Delta S$, z-scored on training statistics, combined as
  $w_1 Z(\Delta V) + w_2 Z(\Delta S)$ and thresholded ($>\tau$) into $y\in\{0,1\}$.
- **§ Model Selection:** "a supervised binary classification task ($y \in \{0,1\}$):
  whether a ticker experiences a composite surge within the subsequent 24 hours."

**Code grounding.** `surge_pipeline/windowing.py` computes
`posting_volume_growth = (forward_count / max(backward_count, 1)) − 1`, and
`surge_pipeline/labelling.py` (report `@lst:surge-labelling`) produces
`surge_label = where(composite > tau, 1, 0)` — the exact target the report describes.

## 2. At what point in time — observation time *t*, forecasting the next 24 h ✔

The report cleanly separates *when the prediction is made* from *what future window it
concerns*:

- **§ Aim (`@sec:aim`):** "predicting per-ticker Reddit surges over a **24-hour horizon**
  from only **backward-looking features available at observation time**."
- **§ Data Representation (`@sec:data-representation`):** windows are measured relative to
  each record's own observation time $t$ — backward $[t-24\text{h}, t)$, forward
  $(t, t+24\text{h}]$ — so the prediction is made at $t$ using only past data and concerns
  the next 24 hours.
- **§ Requirements (G1):** "Forecast a surge in the next 24 hours using only signals
  available when a post is scored."

**Code grounding.** `windowing.py` implements the two windows with `np.searchsorted`:
backward `(t − WINDOW_SECONDS, t)` and forward `(t, t + WINDOW_SECONDS]`, with
`WINDOW_SECONDS` = 24 h. Observation time (prediction point) and forward target window are
therefore distinct in code, matching the report. (This overlaps R3, which independently
passes.)

## 3. For what social-media domain — Reddit financial communities ✔

- **Title / Abstract:** "Reddit Financial Communities"; results on the "`r/wallstreetbets`"
  and "`r/pennystocks`" communities.
- **§ Background (`@sec:background`):** "It focuses on Reddit's financial communities,
  which uniquely combine public, archived, timestamped discussions tied to named stock
  tickers."
- **§ Study Scope:** "two archival 2021 Reddit datasets, `r/pennystocks` and
  `r/wallstreetbets`."
- **Unit of analysis** is fixed as the **record–ticker pair** (§ Data Representation), so
  the domain is bounded not just to a platform but to per-ticker discussion within two
  named 2021 subreddits.

**Code grounding.** The two raw inputs are
`input/raw/r_pennystocks_submissions_reddit.csv` and
`input/raw/r_wallstreetbets_submissions_reddit.csv`; `loader.py` ingests Reddit
submissions and explodes them into record–ticker pairs. (Overlaps R4, which passes.)
