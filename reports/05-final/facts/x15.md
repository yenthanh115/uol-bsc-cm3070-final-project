# X15 — Research claim exceeds experiment

**Severity:** CRITICAL
**Failure mode:** The research claim is generalised beyond what was actually tested (e.g.
"predicts trend emergence" stated as a universal result rather than one bounded to the tested
Reddit / financial / domain / time setting).
**Why dangerous:** Scientifically weak even with correct code.
**Final validation:** Make sure "predicts trend emergence" isn't generalised beyond the tested
Reddit/financial/domain/time setting.
**Assessed:** 2026-09-05
**Status:** **PASS**

X15 is a report-content check: do the stated claims stay within the boundary of what was
experimentally tested — two 2021 Reddit financial subreddits, per-ticker 24-hour surge
classification, three specific models — or do they overreach? Reading the claim-bearing sections
(Abstract, Aim/Contributions, Scope, Assumptions, Conclusion), the report consistently scopes its
claims to the tested setting and repeatedly, explicitly, refuses the broader generalisations. It
does not overclaim.

---

## 1. The headline claim is stated in bounded terms ✔

The central result is never phrased as "trends are predictable" in general. It is tied to the
tested entity, horizon, domain, and period:

- **Aim** (`@sec:aim`): "predicting **per-ticker Reddit surges over a 24-hour horizon** from only
  backward-looking features available at observation time" — entity (ticker), platform (Reddit),
  horizon (24h) all fixed.
- **Abstract**: results are attributed to the specific communities — XGBoost "on the high-volume
  `r/wallstreetbets` community (AUC-ROC 0.892)", RF "on the sparser `r/pennystocks` community
  (0.753)" — and the closing sentence bounds them: "limited by class imbalance, threshold
  calibration, sparse-community uncertainty, and **the use of a single 2021 observation period**."
- **Conclusion / Key Findings**: "surges can be predicted from backward-looking signals alone.
  **How well depends almost entirely on how much data the community generates**" — a
  data-density-conditioned claim, not a universal one.

## 2. Deployment / impact claims are explicitly deflated ✔

The report actively prevents the strongest over-reaches a reader might infer:

- **No market/causal claim**: "A high surge probability means discussion is statistically likely
  to escalate within 24 hours. It does not imply price movement, manipulation, or any recommended
  action" (`@sec:operational-criteria`); Study Scope excludes "trading signals, financial advice,
  or causal claims about market impact."
- **Not autonomous**: Abstract — the trade-off "does not justify autonomous action";
  `@sec:operational-precision` — "Full automation … is not supported by these results."
- **Not universal deployment coverage**: Abstract — "insufficient for broad surveillance or
  moderation coverage."

## 3. Scope and exclusions are stated up front ✔

The "Study Scope and Exclusions" section fixes the experimental boundary and lists what is *not*
claimed: real-time ingestion and production deployment; user/comment-network and cross-platform
effects; multi-class/regression targets; trading signals/advice/causal market claims. This is the
explicit fence X15 asks for.

## 4. Generalisation is treated as a tested limitation, not an assumption ✔

Where the claim *could* be over-generalised — across time and across communities — the report
tests or flags it rather than asserting it:

- **Time**: Assumption 6 ("the 2021 archive is informative beyond its window") is labelled the
  **weakest** assumption; the Conclusion states "2021 may not be representative … Running on 2020
  or 2022 data would matter more for credibility than any model improvement."
- **Community transfer**: cross-dataset transfer is measured, and the report says it "requires
  community-specific recalibration" (Abstract) and analyses the asymmetry (`@sec:cross-community`)
  rather than claiming the model generalises across communities for free.
- **Sparse-community caution**: the pennystocks result is repeatedly qualified ("31 test surges …
  conclusions from pennystocks alone are tentative"; "rests on thin ground").

## 5. Originality claims are bounded ✔

The contribution section explicitly limits comparability and novelty: "Direct comparison with
published baselines is not possible, as no reviewed study predicts surges on the same datasets
with the same temporal protocol. These are **incremental contributions** … each claim grounded in
quantified evidence" (`## Originality and Contribution`). It claims a *methodology and finding for
this setting*, not a general law of trend prediction.

## 6. Template framing is adapted, not overstated ✔

The report is explicit that it *adapts* the CM3005 "trend emergence" template to a narrower,
better-defined target ("This project instead targets *surges*…", `@sec:prediction-target`), so it
does not inherit or assert the broad "predicts trend emergence" claim the checklist warns about —
it replaces it with a concrete, testable surge definition.

## Status

**X15: PASS.** The research claim does not exceed the experiment. The central result is stated as
"per-ticker Reddit surges can be predicted over a 24-hour horizon, with performance conditioned on
data density," attributed to the two specific 2021 communities actually tested, and the report
explicitly refuses the tempting over-generalisations: it makes no price-movement, manipulation, or
causal market claim, disclaims autonomous or broad-coverage deployment, flags the single-2021-year
and sparse-community limits, treats temporal and cross-community generalisation as tested/limited
rather than assumed, and frames its contributions as incremental and setting-specific. The scope
and exclusions are stated up front and honoured throughout.

### Minor observations (not blocking)

- The three user groups (surveillance/compliance, quant researchers, moderators) are framed as
  *motivating* scenarios and their applicability is explicitly graded in `@sec:operational-precision`
  ("insufficient as standalone surveillance"; "well-suited" only for quant research). This keeps the
  user-facing framing within evidence and does not constitute an over-claim.
- The reusability remark ("framework is reusable for any timestamped prediction problem",
  `## Originality and Contribution`) is a statement about the *methodology's portability*, not a
  claim that the *empirical surge result* generalises; it is phrased as reusable machinery rather
  than a proven cross-domain result, so it stays within X15. Still, a one-line qualifier that
  portability is untested outside this study would make the boundary even crisper.
