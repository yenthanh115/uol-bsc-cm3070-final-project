## Research gaps identified

1. **Prediction target mismatch** — Literature focuses on eventual outcomes (final popularity, market direction) rather than detecting the onset of rapid growth within a bounded time window
2. **Single-signal approaches** — Each strand demonstrates one feature category; few studies integrate temporal + content + sentiment + structural signals
3. **Limited finance-specific work** — General social media platforms studied (YouTube, Digg, Facebook); little work on finance-specific discussion platforms

## Structure decisions

- Organised review thematically (by methodology) rather than chronologically
- Each section follows: context → key study → contribution → limitations → relevance to this project
- Comparative analysis section synthesises across all strands rather than treating them in isolation
- Conclusion explicitly connects gaps to proposed project design

## Observations and reflections

- The literature is surprisingly siloed — temporal prediction papers rarely cite sentiment work, and vice versa. This reinforces the value of an integrated approach.
- Bollen et al. [4] is extremely highly cited but has significant methodological weaknesses (short evaluation period, no OOS validation). Need to handle this carefully — acknowledge influence while noting limitations.
- Cascade prediction (Cheng et al.) is the strongest methodologically but relies on platform-specific network data not available in Reddit's discussion format.
- TextBlob (chosen for this project) shares many of the lexicon-based limitations identified in the Bollen critique. This is a known trade-off — simplicity and reproducibility vs accuracy. Documented in risk register.
