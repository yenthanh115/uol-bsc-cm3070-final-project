# Research Journal — 2026-06-11

## Session focus

Critical review of the composite surge formula in the preliminary report (v0.26, Section 1.5).

## Concern: Scale asymmetry in composite surge metric

The current composite formula is:

> *composite = posting_volume_growth + |sentiment_change|*

This formulation has four interrelated problems:

### 1. Different scales

Posting volume growth is an unbounded ratio — a ticker going from 1 post to 10 posts in the next 24 hours yields a value of 9.0. Meanwhile |sentiment_change| is bounded by [0, 2.0] because TextBlob polarity ranges [-1, +1]. The two components are not commensurable: one lives on [0, ∞) and the other on [0, 2].

### 2. Volume dominates

Because of the scale mismatch, for any record with meaningful volume growth (say > 3), the sentiment component becomes negligible noise. Example: a ticker with posting_volume_growth = 10 and |sentiment_change| = 0 gets composite = 10 (clear surge). A ticker with posting_volume_growth = 0.5 and |sentiment_change| = 2.0 (maximum possible sentiment flip) gets composite = 2.5. The metric is effectively a volume-only metric with a small sentiment perturbation — undermining the claim that this is a genuinely composite target.

### 3. Difficult to interpret

What does composite = 4.5 mean? It could be posting tripled with no sentiment shift, or posting doubled with a moderate sentiment shift. Because the components carry unequal implicit weight, the combined number has no stable interpretation.

### 4. Threshold becomes arbitrary

Setting any fixed threshold primarily controls sensitivity to the volume component, because volume dominates the composite value. The threshold sensitivity analysis (Section 4.7) will effectively sweep volume thresholds with a tiny sentiment bonus rather than sweeping a genuinely composite operating point.

## Assessment

These are real methodological concerns, not cosmetic. The report acknowledges the scale asymmetry in the "Scale considerations" paragraph but defers resolution to experimental analysis. This means the entire Phase 1 vs Phase 2 comparison — the project's core contribution — rests on a formula where sentiment may be structurally unable to influence the label. If sentiment never flips labels because volume always dominates, Phase 2 will trivially match Phase 1, and the conclusion will be "sentiment doesn't help" — when the real conclusion should be "the formula doesn't let sentiment help."

## Potential solution: Z-score normalisation

Replace the raw additive formula with standardised components:

```
z_volume    = (posting_volume_growth - μ_vol) / σ_vol
z_sentiment = (|sentiment_change| - μ_sent) / σ_sent

composite = (w₁ × z_volume) + (w₂ × z_sentiment)
```

Where μ and σ are computed from the training set only (to avoid leakage), and w₁ = w₂ = 0.5 by default.

**Benefits:**
- Both components become zero-mean, unit-variance — neither dominates by construction
- Threshold becomes interpretable: "N standard deviations above typical combined activity"
- Weight split (w₁/w₂) becomes a principled hyperparameter for sensitivity analysis
- Phase 1 = w₂=0; Phase 2 = w₂=0.5; weight sweep adds w₂ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}
- Threshold sensitivity becomes meaningful — uniformly affects sensitivity to both signals

**Implementation note:** μ and σ must be computed from the training partition only. Test records are normalised using training-set statistics.
