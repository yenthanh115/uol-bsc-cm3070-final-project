# Research Journal — 2026-06-08

## Session focus

Preliminary report review and revision. Identified and resolved several conceptual and structural weaknesses through iterative critique and improvement.

## Issues raised and resolved

### 1. Missing citations and reference list

The preliminary report had no formal references section and no in-text citations. Added a numbered reference list (IEEE format) and integrated citations throughout — particularly in the problem statement, motivation, feature design rationale, and evaluation strategy.

### 2. No formal surge definition

The report used "surge" without defining it precisely. Added Section 1.5 with the formal composite metric definition (`engagement_growth + |sentiment_change|`), threshold justification, and rationale for why 2.0 is the default.

### 3. Weak stakeholder/domain justification

Problem statement was generic. Expanded to identify four specific stakeholder groups (analysts, regulators, researchers, platform operators) with concrete reasons why automated surge prediction matters to each.

### 4. Missing architecture and feature design

Methodology section listed pipeline stages but didn't show the design. Added system architecture diagram (Mermaid), feature design table with 7 features (type, description, literature-backed rationale), and composite target design with step-by-step computation.

### 5. Evaluation strategy too shallow

Only listed metrics without discussing how to draw valid conclusions. Added:
- Bootstrap confidence intervals (95%, 1000 iterations)
- Multiple-seed evaluation (5 seeds)
- McNemar's test for pairwise model comparison
- Full threshold sensitivity analysis ({1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0})

### 6. Threshold justification (why 2.0?)

Challenge: why not 1.5 or 3.0? Resolved by explaining the scale of each component (engagement growth is a ratio, sentiment bounded by polarity range), what a threshold of 2.0 practically requires, and noting it's subject to sensitivity analysis.

### 7. "What is a record?" — unit of analysis ambiguity

The report said "for each record" without defining what a record is or whether we're predicting per-record, per-thread, or per-subreddit. Resolved by adding a full Unit of Analysis section distinguishing records, ticker-windows, and threads.

### 8. Per-ticker vs per-subreddit surge scoping

Critical conceptual issue: the original design computed surges against ALL future records (subreddit-level), but the title implies per-discussion/per-stock prediction. Resolved by scoping the prediction window to same-ticker records. A record about $TSLA is evaluated against future $TSLA activity only.

### 9. Why combine engagement and sentiment?

Challenge: why not treat them as separate prediction tasks? Resolved with three-part justification (neither alone is sufficient, literature supports interaction, single target simplifies evaluation) plus the phased experimental approach (engagement-only baseline → composite).

### 10. Literature review was descriptive, not critical

"Paper A did X" format doesn't satisfy academic critical evaluation requirements. Rewrote to assess methodological strengths, validity threats, generalisability limitations, and specific relevance to this project for each cited work.

### 11. Success criteria too conservative

Single threshold (AUC > 0.60) was pass/fail. Expanded to three tiers: minimum (0.60), target (0.70), stretch (0.80), plus functional, analytical, and academic criteria categories.

### 12. Dataset source not mentioned

Added Section 2.2 documenting the Kaggle Reddit Finance Data source, 9 subreddits, ~1.38M records, primary dataset selection (pennystocks), and surge viability analysis from EDA.

### 14. Per-ticker sparsity risk identified and mitigated

Added Risk #9 to the risk register: per-ticker scoping (DEC-002) means that tickers with very few posts in a 24-hour window produce unstable or meaningless surge labels. Mitigation: enforce minimum record count (N ≥ 3) within the ticker window; exclude or flag records below this threshold; report exclusion rate; conduct sensitivity analysis on minimum-N during EDA. Recorded as DEC-011.

### 13. Composite metric scale mismatch

Identified that `engagement_growth + |sentiment_change|` combines an unbounded ratio (engagement) with a bounded value capped at ~2.0 (sentiment). In practice, the composite is likely engagement-dominated. Rather than applying arbitrary normalisation, decided to investigate empirically: the phased comparison (Phase 1 vs Phase 2) and threshold sensitivity analysis will quantify the actual relative influence. Added explicit "Scale considerations" paragraph to Section 1.5 of the preliminary report and recorded as DEC-010.

### 14. Ticker sparsity elevated to formal risk

Recognised that per-ticker sparsity is not just a limitation footnote but likely the biggest implementation risk. Most of the 2,912 tickers in pennystocks probably have very few posts — ticker frequency in social media follows a power-law distribution. A 24-hour window for a low-frequency ticker may contain 0–2 future records, making the surge metric numerically unstable (a single outlier flips the label). Elevated to Risk #9 (High likelihood, High impact) with mitigation: enforce minimum N ≥ 3 records in the forward window, exclude records that fail, and quantify the exclusion rate during EDA. Expanded the Section 1.4 limitation bullet into a detailed paragraph with cross-reference to the risk. Recorded as DEC-011.
